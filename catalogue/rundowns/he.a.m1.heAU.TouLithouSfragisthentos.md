# Τοῦ λίθου σφραγισθέντος

**What it is.** The Resurrectional apolytikion of the First Mode. An *automelon*: its melody is a model that other apolytikia are sung to.

**What it says.** The stone was sealed by the Jews and soldiers guarded the tomb, yet on the third day You rose, O Saviour, granting life to the world. So the powers of heaven cried out: *Glory to Your resurrection, O Christ; glory to Your kingdom; glory to Your dispensation, only Lover of mankind.* Three "glories" at the end — the melody rises with them.

**Mode.** First Mode, diatonic, from Pa. Bright and steady; a good first apolytikion to learn because its phrases are short and repeat.

**When and where.** This is the week's Resurrectional apolytikion, so it comes round every eighth week. Saturday Great Vespers: after *Now let your servant depart* and the Trisagion prayers, just before the dismissal, followed by its Theotokion. Sunday Orthros: after *God is the Lord*, sung twice, then the Theotokion. Divine Liturgy: after the Small Entrance, before the saints' apolytikia and the kontakion. It is the hymn a congregation is most likely to sing along with.
