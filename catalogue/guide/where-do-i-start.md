# Where do I start?

Here, if you have never chanted before. Nothing is locked, nothing is scored, and you can
open anything in any order — this is only the order that works.

## Do this first

Open [Scales → Pl. 4](/scales/pl4). Tap **Play** and listen to the ladder go up and down.
Then tap **Tune**, allow the microphone, and sing one of the rungs back: the needle shows
how close you landed. Ten minutes of that is a good first sitting.

## The three sections

- **Scales** — one ladder per mode: the eight notes Νη Πα Βου Γα Δη Κε Ζω Νη′, the distance
  between each pair, a drone to sing against and a tuner that hears you. This is where the
  ear is built.
- **Notation** — the neumes, the signs a Byzantine score is written in, learned in families.
  Not built yet.
- **Library** — the hymns, each with a recording, the words and the score.

The path is **scales → notation → hymns**, and the order is not arbitrary: a neume tells you
to move *up two* or *down one*, never which note to sing. Until the ladder is in your ear,
the score has nothing to say to you.

## The modes, in the order to learn them

**Pl. 4 → 1 → Pl. 1 → 4 → 2 → Pl. 2 → 3 → Grave**

The first four are diatonic — one set of notes, read from a different starting rung each
time. Pl. 4 reads it from Νη, the plainest place to stand, so it comes first. Then the two
chromatic modes, with their wide step in the middle, and last the two enharmonic. Only
Pl. 4 is built so far.

## The hymns, in the order to learn them

1. The fixed hymns of the Divine Liturgy — what is sung every Sunday.
   [Holy, holy, holy](/library/holy-holy-holy) is the one here now.
2. The eight Resurrectional apolytikia, one in each mode.
3. The fixed hymns of Vespers and Orthros.

The long, slow Papadic pieces — the Cherubic Hymn, the Communion hymn — carry a *not for
beginners* tag when they arrive. Nothing stops you opening them; they are simply not where
to start.

A **Beginner** tag marks the mode and the hymns to begin with.
